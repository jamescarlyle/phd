Reconfigurable Complementary Spike Encoding in a Single Memristor for Dual-Pathway Neuromorphic Perception

The authors present a study entitled "Reconfigurable Complementary Spike Encoding in a Single Memristor for Dual-Pathway Neuromorphic Perception", which claims that a single electrically reconfigurable Pt/CeO2/V volatile memristive spike encoder can achieve deterministic and stochastic encoding. This claim is supported by electrical characterisation, XPS analysis, and spike rate measurements. The work is interesting and has significance for spiking neural network processing for both context and detailed analysis. However, several presentational issues exist, and some technical questions are insufficiently addressed.

Technical concerns
1. The study makes several claims to reduced energy consumption though stochastic spike generation (page 2 line 22, page 15 line 9). Supplemental page 10/11 describes how this claim is derived through lower spike voltages needed in the stochastic regime, based on a relationship between voltage and power, but does indicate how many spikes were processed in both stochastic and deterministic regimes in order to achieve the classification accuracy achieved in facial expression tests. Typically, stochastic spike processing requires more spikes in a wider window in order for rates to stabilise. Since total energy consumption depends on energy per spike as well as the number of spikes, so it would be helpful to extend the energy calculation to include this.
2. The study states on page 3 line 3 that biological vision employs multiple encoding behaviours to accomplish different perceptual functions, and on page 5 in the description and diagram of Figure 1 a), that deterministic coding is used for precise facial expression detection in the Inferior Temporal (IT) Complex. While the brain uses deterministic (periodic) coding for internal pacemaking, e.g. in dopaminergic neurons or central pattern generators, can the authors provide evidence that deterministic coding is also used in pattern recognition in the IT Complex? A 2017 paper by Wang et al., "Human amygdala parametrically encodes intensity of emotions", argues that emotion intensity is correlated with spike rate, but not that the spikes are necessarily periodic.
3. On page 3 line 14, the authors state that the encoder is reconfigurable. Can they indicate whether any form of device reset is required when switching between regimes, or whether bias adjustment itself is sufficient? Also, is the wording on page 5 line 7 correct in saying that the encoder can produce stochastic and deterministic encoding _simultaneously_, rather than a single device can easily switch encoding type?
4. The authors provide data of cycle-to-cycle stability across 100 voltage sweeps on page 6 line 11. Given the proposed application of the memristor, of encoding (e.g. images) for neuromorphic perception, a much greater number of LRS-HRS transitions would be required. Do the authors have any longer term endurance stability data?
5. For the face detection and facial expression simulation description in the text accompanying figure 5 on page 15, can the authors indicate if the device to device and cycle to cycle variability seen in device characterisation was incorporated into their simulation?

Minor presentational issues

6. On page 6 line 19 figure 2 d) shows device to device variability, with a standard deviation of V_th of 0.0114. Assuming this is a device to device measurement, can the authors confirm the plot depicts this value of standard deviation, since the graph figures indicate a V_th of 0.9 for device 5 and 1.3 for device 3.
7. On page 6 line 19 the accompanying text for figure 2 e) states that current rapidly increases when voltage exceeds V_th, but the accompanying graph actually shows the current falling during this period from 0.8A to 0A. A related issue exists in Figure 3 e) where the current (y) axis shows spikes of falling current unless the axis value is read downwards. Perhaps the authors could clarify the direction of axis values. Supplemental information page 3 figure S5 shows a drop in current during the transition from off-state to on-state. Can the authors confirm this lowering of current?
8. Figure 4 e) and f) use inconsistent colours for voltages; for example, in e) green is the lowest voltage; in f) it is the second-highest.

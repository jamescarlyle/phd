# How robust is on-chip learning in memristor-based synapse and neuron circuits to analog variability?​

Memristor-based synapse and neuron devices could offer dense analog computation, but
they exhibit significant cycle-to-cycle and device-to-device variability that can degrade on-chip
learning ability. This project asks how robust such on-chip learning is to analog variability,
combining device characterisation, variability-aware simulation, and hardware validation. ​

The first phase measures volatile and non-volatile behaviour of newly-fabricated ZnO and SiNx
memristors, extracting operating and variability data for synapse and neuron roles.  This poster
shows initial pulse variability findings from Ag/ZnO memristors.​

The second phase incorporates the variability data into a multilayer spiking neural network
simulation using established on-chip learning rules such as spike-timing-dependent or
voltage-dependent plasticity (STDP and VDSP), incorporating variability parameters to study
the impact of non-idealities on classification accuracy. ​

The final phase constructs a hardware neural network from packaged synapse and neuron
arrays and CMOS interface circuits, verifies that hardware variability matches the earlier
measurements, and compares training performance against the simulation.​
